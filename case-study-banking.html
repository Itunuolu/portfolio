01 · Challenge

Exception handling was creating operational friction.

Transaction exceptions are inevitable in high-volume banking. The business problem is how quickly teams can identify, classify, assign, investigate, resolve and monitor them.

Transformation thesis: do not add more people to a broken workflow. Redesign the workflow first, then use technology to support the improved process.
Manual classificationAnalysts repeatedly determine exception type and cause.
Unclear ownershipCases can move between teams before the right owner is found.
Limited visibilityManagers lack a single view of open, ageing and escalated cases.

02 · Current state

The largest inefficiencies sat between detection and ownership: repetitive investigation, unclear routing, manual escalation and fragmented reporting.

03 · Diagnosis

Root cause, not symptoms.

PeopleHeavy reliance on employee experience and inconsistent handling approaches.
ProcessMultiple handoffs, manual escalation and repetitive steps.
TechnologyLimited rules-based routing, consolidated visibility and workflow automation.
InformationException data distributed across sources with inconsistent categorisation.
ControlSLA breaches could become visible too late for proactive intervention.
ReportingManagement information required manual consolidation.

04 · Future state

TO-BE process: route intelligently, escalate by rule.

01Detect once
02Create case
03Classify & prioritise
04Route by rule
05Investigate
06Resolve & measure
Design principle: if a case approaches its SLA threshold, the workflow should trigger visibility and escalation before the breach — not after.

05 · Requirements

Translate the future state into testable requirements.

Functional requirements

  • Automatically create an exception case for qualifying failures.
  • Assign a unique case identifier and structured exception category.
  • Route cases to the appropriate team based on business rules.
  • Support statuses, ownership, comments and case history.
  • Trigger notifications and escalation based on SLA thresholds.
  • Provide operational dashboards and search/filter capability.

Non-functional requirements

  • Role-based access and auditability.
  • Reliable case creation and persistence.
  • Scalability for transaction and exception volume.
  • Usability for high-frequency operational tasks.
  • Acceptable response times for case queues and dashboards.
  • Controlled configuration of SLA and routing rules.

Example user story

As an Operations Manager, I want to identify exceptions approaching their SLA so that I can intervene before they become overdue.

Acceptance: when the remaining resolution time reaches the defined threshold, the case is visually highlighted, the owner is notified, and the manager can see it in the escalation view.

07 · Outcome

Better process. Better visibility. Better decisions.

The real-world process optimisation initiative that inspired this case study contributed to approximately a 20% improvement in process efficiency.

What this demonstrates: current-state analysis, stakeholder alignment, root-cause thinking, requirements engineering, user stories, acceptance criteria, UAT thinking and KPI-led measurement.
